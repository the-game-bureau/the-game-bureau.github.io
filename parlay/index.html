How it works

All you need is the Parlay Squares Notebook and a pen.

1

Buy in before kickoff

There are always 12 Prognosticator numbers, PROG #1 to #12. Each costs the same, and one person can own several. The money makes the pot, and you pick how many drives it pays for: 12, 24, 36 or 48.

2

Claim a square before every drive

Before each drive starts, every prognosticator, in the listed order, picks one of 12 different parlays predicting how the drive will end.

3

The drive picks the winner

When the drive ends, exactly one parlay square matches. Note the winner for later payout.

The 12 squares

3 groups. 2 ways each. Odd or even.

TOUCHDOWNRun or pass, then the scorer's jersey number decides odd or even.
FIELD GOALGood or miss, then the kick distance in yards decides odd or even. A block is a miss.
TURN OVERNo touchdown or field goal try. Punt or other, then the last two digits of the game clock decide. 00 is even.

Other means an interception, a lost fumble, a turnover on downs, a safety, or the end of the half or game.

    Rules

    Everything you need at the table.

    BEFORE KICKOFF

    1. Plan the game. Play with 2 to 12 people. Choose to fund 12, 24, 36, or 48 drives.
    2. Assign all 12 PROG #s. Each number costs the same. A person can own more than one.
    3. Pay up front. Pay for your numbers once to fill the pot. Write each owner's name on the notebook's Prognosticators page. Keep those numbers all game.

    EACH DRIVE

    1. Claim a square. Follow the printed pick order. Each PROG # gets one pick. Write that number in an open square on the drive's sheet.
    2. Close picks. Finish before the kickoff or snap that starts the drive.
    3. Find the winner. When the drive ends, write the result number in the matching square's tab. Its owner wins that drive's payout: the pot divided by the number of drives funded.
    4. End the game. Play until the pot runs out or the football game ends. Split any leftover money evenly.

    Pick order. Start with #1 through #12, then reverse on the next drive. Start each new pair one number higher and wrap after #12.

    Blocked kicks. A block counts as a miss. If a blocked kick is returned for a touchdown, use Touchdown Run and the returner's jersey number.

    Game clock. For a turn over, use the last two digits on the game clock when the drive ends. 00 is even.

    Unusual plays. Before kickoff, agree how to handle fake punts and kicks, penalties, and other defensive touchdowns.
