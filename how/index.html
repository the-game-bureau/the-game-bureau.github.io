Browse Games

Five-second setup

Open the link, you're in. No app, no account, no Bluetooth pairing, no "please update before continuing." The shortest path from idea to playing.

Block by block

Every clue ties to something specific on the route — the old painted ad, the wrong house number, the bench nobody sits on. The route is the research, and the clues reward looking up.

Written by humans

No procedural generation, no AI-stitched trivia. Each route is plotted, written, and rewritten by a person who walked it — which is why new games take weeks, not minutes.

Safety

The street outranks the screen. Stop in a safe place before reading clues, answering prompts, crossing streets, or taking photos.

Access

Nothing to install. Games run in the browser, so you can open the link and let the route take over from there.

Payment

No ticket booth. When a paid game asks you to unlock the rest, checkout returns you to the same spot in the route.

Common questions

Do I need event tickets to play?
No. Many of our games are designed around an event — an away game, a convention, a fan weekend — but the game is its own thing. Tickets to whatever's happening that weekend aren't required.
Can I play any time, or only during the event?
Both. The event is the hook, but the clues reference the city — not the calendar. You can play the same game any day of the year.
How long does a game take?
About 90 minutes for most groups. Longer if you stop for food, photos, or get genuinely stuck on a clue.
How many people can play?
Two to eight is the sweet spot. One phone runs the whole group — no logging in, no syncing, no installing.
Do I need to download an app?
No. The game opens in your browser when you tap the link. No app store, no account, no install.
What if I get stuck on a clue?
Every clue has a hint built in. If you're still stuck, you can skip ahead — the game keeps going.
Are these written by AI?
No. Every route is plotted, walked, written, and rewritten by a person. That's why new games take weeks — and why the clues reference things only a human who walked the block would notice.
