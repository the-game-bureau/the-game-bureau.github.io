Maintain the city soundtracks

Soundtracks Admin

Everything you need to keep the 62 city cassettes fresh: how the data is wired, how to collect the song suggestions listeners email in, and the exact steps to add a track.

1 How it works

The public page /sound/ renders one cassette case per city and fetches everything at runtime — nothing is hard-coded in the HTML.

  • City identity (name, label, sort order, geo badges, tape colors) lives in the Supabase public.cities table, keyed by slug.
  • The songs live in sound/soundtracks.json in the repo, keyed by city_slug (which matches public.cities.slug).
  • Editing songs is just editing soundtracks.json and deploying — there is no build step. The page re-fetches the JSON on load.

Full reference: sound/README.md.

2 Song suggestions arrive by email

Every city cassette has a “Suggest a song for the {City} Soundtrack” button. It opens the listener's mail app pre-addressed to soundtrack@thegamebureau.com with this shape:

To:      soundtrack@thegamebureau.com
Subject: Song suggestion for {City}

Suggested song for {City}

Song title:
Artist:
Why it fits the city:

Review them in Gmail

Open the inbox for soundtrack@thegamebureau.com (or wherever it forwards) and search:

  • to:soundtrack@thegamebureau.com — everything sent to the address.
  • subject:(Song suggestion) — just the suggestion emails.
  • Narrow to one city with subject:"Song suggestion for New Orleans".
Tip: skim for the Song title, Artist, and the city in the subject. A good suggestion is a real Spotify track, ideally a local artist / stadium cue / a song that names the place. Then follow section 3 to add it.

3 Add a track

  1. Pick the song. From a suggestion or your own curation. The recipe per city: local / hometown artists, sports & stadium cues, and songs that name the place. Aim for 10 songs, max 2 per artist.
  2. Get its Spotify track ID. Open the track in Spotify → Share → Copy Song Link. You get https://open.spotify.com/track/2xZPpUIcajScKX99PH0fD9?…. The ID is the 22 characters right after /track/ (drop everything from the ? on).
  3. Confirm the track & explicit flag. Make sure it's the real studio version (not a karaoke/“made famous by” re-record). Note whether Spotify shows an E Explicit tag — if so you'll set "explicit": true so the cassette shows the “E” tile.
  4. Add it to sound/soundtracks.json. Find the city by its city_slug and append a song object to its songs array: 
    {
  "city_slug": "new-orleans",
  "songs": [
    …existing songs…,
    {
      "title": "Iko Iko",
      "artist": "The Dixie Cups",
      "spotifyId": "2xZPpUIcajScKX99PH0fD9",
      "blurb": "Mardi Gras Indian chant from a homegrown girl group",
      "explicit": false
    }
  ]
}
  5. Commit & deploy. No build step — the page fetches soundtracks.json at load, so the new track appears on the next visit. Hard-refresh to bust cache.

Song fields

FieldRequiredWhat it is
titleyesTrack name as you want it shown on the cassette label.
artistyesPerforming artist.
spotifyIdrecommended22-char Spotify track ID. If empty, the tape falls back to a Spotify search for “artist title” (still plays, one extra tap).
blurbrecommendedShort “why it fits the city” line (~6–9 words) shown on the label.
explicitoptionaltrue to paint the “E” tile. Omit or false for clean tracks.
Editing or removing a track is the same file: change or delete the object in that city's songs array and deploy.

4 Adding a brand-new city

  1. Add the city row to Supabase public.cities — required for /sound/: slug, city, label, sort_order, and structured geo fields where available. Optional sound fields: sound_playlist_id, sound_accent, sound_secondary.
  2. Add a matching entry to soundtracks.json using the same city_slug, with its ~10 songs.

Without a public.cities row the city won't appear on the grid even if it's in soundtracks.json.
